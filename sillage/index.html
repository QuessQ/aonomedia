The most personal thing you wear, you still can't try online.

01 / READOUT

A $100 billion category that people still shop for by guessing.

Demand is surging. The way people discover fragrance hasn't changed in decades, and nobody online has fixed it.

The market

$106B

The problem, in three parts

Demand moved online. The nose didn't.

01

You can't smell through a screen.

Discovery still happens at a counter. Shoppers find scents in stores at roughly 3.5 times the rate they find them on social media, and they're twice as likely to buy in a department store as online.

~30% won't buy a scent they haven't smelled5
60% discover in store vs 17% via social6
02

You don't have the words.

Product pages describe fragrance in perfumer's vocabulary: ambroxan, iso e super, aldehydes. Most people can't even name everyday smells reliably.

English speakers correctly named common odors like coffee about half the time7
03

There's too much to choose from.

Launches are up about 25-fold in thirty years, while testing in person tops out after a few sprays. Your nose adapts within minutes, so the fifth strip smells weaker than the first.

US assortment 17.8K SKUs, +23% in a year13
Perceived intensity drops within 120 seconds10
57% spent under $50 on their last bottle; luxury sits at $250–350614Dupe sales +79% in 2024845% just repurchase the same one or two scents6

People don't lack options. What they lack is confidence before they buy.

The gap

Everyone in the market is solving a different problem.

Personal to you Same for everyone One brand / B2B Every brand Jo Malone Advisor YSL Scent-Sation Osmo · Givaudan · IFF Fragrantica Parfumo Scentbird Perfumist Indie LLM apps Sillage
Databases with no personalization

Fragrantica is one of the most-visited beauty sites on the web, and it shows every visitor the same pages.15

Brand AI that only recommends its own bottles

Jo Malone launched a Gemini-powered scent advisor in December 2025. It can only point you to Jo Malone.12

Industry AI built for perfumers

Osmo has raised $130M to map smell digitally, but its customers are fragrance houses. It has no consumer product.11

Nobody owns the shopper's side

No one offers a cross-brand advisor that understands your taste, ships the sample to prove it, and gets smarter every time you wear one.

The answer

Sillage translates memory into molecules.

Rain on hot pavementA fig tree in AugustA cedar closet

Scent DNA

You describe moments you remember, not notes you can't name. Sillage maps them to a profile across eight accord families. No perfume vocabulary needed.

Drydown, on you

The scent on a paper strip isn't the scent on you four hours later. Sillage predicts how each fragrance unfolds on your skin, hour by hour, before you spend anything.

The wear-test loop

A 2 ml sample arrives at your door. Sillage checks in at one, four and eight hours. Every rating sharpens your profile, and your blind-buy confidence climbs.

What we're holding back

How memories map to molecules, how we model skin, and what the wear-test data compounds into. We'll walk through it in person.

Why a retailer can't just add this

Neutral is the product.

A retailer's advisor

Recommends what's on its own shelf.

A brand's advisor

Recommends itself.

Sillage

Recommends what fits you, across every house, and learns from what you actually keep wearing.

The moat is the wear-test data: what real people chose to keep wearing at hour eight, on their own skin. Nobody else collects it, and every sample shipped makes the model harder to copy.
